Understanding Database Index Types: B-Trees, Hash, and GiST

Published on August 8, 2023 · 4 min read · 2,410 views

Indexes are the secret sauce of query performance. In relational databases like PostgreSQL and SQL Server, an index acts as a lookup helper, allowing the database engine to find specific rows without scanning the entire table from top to bottom.

However, indexes are not one-size-fits-all. Choosing the wrong index type can inflate your database size, slow down insert/update queries, and do absolutely nothing to speed up your searches.

1. B-Tree Indexes

The **B-Tree** (Balanced Tree) is the default and most widely used index type. It maintains sorted data in a self-balancing hierarchical structure, making it ideal for range queries (e.g. WHERE age BETWEEN 18 AND 30) and sorting (ORDER BY).

A B-Tree index handles matches, range lookups, and inequality operations seamlessly because of its sorted tree traversal: O(log N) lookup complexity.

2. Hash Indexes

Unlike B-Trees, **Hash indexes** only support simple equality operators (=). They build a flat hash table of values, maps them to row pointers, and offers constant-time O(1) lookups.

Use Hash indexes when you only need to match exact strings or IDs, but keep in mind they cannot speed up sorting or range evaluations.

3. GiST and GIN Indexes (PostgreSQL)

**GiST** (Generalized Search Tree) and **GIN** (Generalized Inverted Index) are specialized indexes used in PostgreSQL:

  • GiST: Ideal for geometric shapes, coordinates, and range types (e.g. coordinates in a maps app).
  • GIN: Best for full-text search, arrays, and JSONB structures. It indexes sub-keys and elements inside documents.
